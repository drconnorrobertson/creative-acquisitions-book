AI for Business Acquisition Research: A Buyer Workflow

Turn business listings and authorized seller records into an organized acquisition research file.

Original website companion resource. Published October 9, 2026. This guide is not an excerpt or a claim about the original book chapters.

Start with a narrow research question

Ask what you need to learn before spending more time on a deal. Early research may establish whether the industry, size, geography and management requirements fit your buy box. Later research may compare customer concentration, operating capacity or financing assumptions. Give AI one stage and one decision at a time. A general request to evaluate the whole acquisition encourages a summary that blends verified facts with guesses.

Keep public and seller information separate

Public research can describe products, locations and visible positioning. It does not establish revenue, customer retention or seller motivation. Seller records can support financial and operational findings, subject to reconciliation. Use an approved workspace for confidential information and follow the seller agreement. Create separate sections for public observations, seller representations and verified records. Label each statement so a reader can understand its evidentiary strength.

Use a consistent acquisition brief

Include business model, customer types, recurring revenue characteristics, staffing, owner responsibilities, financial periods, financing needs and transition requirements. For each category, ask the model for the finding, source and next question. An absent customer renewal schedule should become a document request. It should not become a statement that revenue is recurring just because the business sells subscriptions.

Prompt for evidence, not a verdict

Use: Prepare an acquisition research brief from these supplied documents. Cite the document and page for each factual finding. Separate verified records, seller representations and public observations. Mark unavailable information explicitly. Produce five questions for the next seller meeting, ordered by how strongly the answers could change operating feasibility. Do not make a purchase recommendation, estimate unprovided financial data or infer personal motives.

Move research into the deal process

Assign each question to an owner and stage: initial call, document request, specialist review or final approval. Track whether the answer changes price, structure, staffing or transition timing. AI can maintain a draft issue list, while the deal team controls conclusions and seller communications. A complete research cycle produces an updated acquisition brief, a narrower set of questions and a specific decision about the next stage.
