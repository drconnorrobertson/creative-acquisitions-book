Review a Small-Business Acquisition Beyond Headline Earnings

A business listing can make a purchase look simple: a price, an earnings figure and a reason the seller wants to leave. Those details are a starting point for questions. They do not establish what a buyer could withdraw, what work ownership would require, or whether a particular transaction fits the buyer's circumstances.

Separate reported earnings from cash available to an owner

Ask who prepared the earnings figure, which entity and period it describes, and which records support it. Request a reconciliation of proposed adjustments rather than treating an adjusted headline as money available after closing. Keep an unresolved adjustment visible until its basis has been reviewed.

Dividing an earnings figure by a purchase price produces a ratio of those two inputs. Calling that ratio an annual investment return skips questions about financing payments, ongoing cash requirements, replacement spending, taxes and the work the owner performs. This article does not estimate any buyer's return.

Make the owner's role explicit

Write down the seller's recurring duties, hours, customer relationships and decision responsibilities. Which duties would the buyer perform personally? Which would require another person, and what evidence supports the proposed cost and availability of that person? A plan to hire a manager is different from an accepted arrangement with a defined role.

Ownership also leaves external dependencies. Ask about customer concentration, supplier arrangements, premises, key staff and any permissions relevant to the specific operation. A buyer's ability to make decisions does not establish control over demand, costs or the wider economy.

Keep purchase funding and operating needs separate

List proposed cash at closing separately from the cash the business may need while operating. Identify the source, timing and conditions attached to each funding assumption. Ask which payments or expenditures are absent from the headline earnings number and which remain unknown.

A historical profitable period does not confirm cash available on the first day after a transfer. Compare the periods covered by the records with the proposed handover date. Keep proposed changes to staffing, pricing or marketing labeled as plans; this article supplies no estimate of their effects on value or earnings.

Review this seller and these documents

A broad story about retiring owners does not establish the motivation, terms or price of an individual seller. Record what the seller has actually stated, which terms are documented and which are still proposals. Do not substitute a demographic narrative for evidence about the specific opportunity.

The SBA's buy-an-existing-business section within its planning guidance, read October 30, 2026, raises questions about money for purchase and management, the buyer's talents and lifestyle, contracts, leases and existing cash flow. It describes due diligence and professional assistance. That limited context supports preparing questions; it does not assess a business or confirm financing eligibility.

Give unanswered questions an owner

For each material assumption, record the document needed, the question to resolve and the person responsible for reviewing it. Keep accounting, legal, lending and tax questions tied to the actual documents and circumstances for the appropriate adviser. No universal tax saving, loan term, approval or transaction outcome is assumed here.

Questions before a transaction review

Does earnings divided by price establish an annual return?

No. That ratio compares two stated inputs. It does not resolve financing payments, operating cash needs, replacement spending, taxes or owner work.

Does historical profit confirm cash available after closing?

No. Ask which period and entity the records cover, what cash requirements remain, and how the proposed handover changes the assumptions.

What should I ask about the seller’s role?

List recurring duties and relationships, then identify who would perform them after a transfer. Keep a proposed replacement role separate from a confirmed arrangement.

What should happen to an unresolved assumption?

Record the missing evidence, the question and the person responsible for review. Keep the answer unknown until that review provides a basis to change it.
