The Quality of Earnings Report: Your Best Defense Against a Bad Acquisition

A quality-of-earnings (QoE) review tests financial information and proposed earnings adjustments within an agreed scope. It can identify inconsistencies and questions that affect an acquisition decision. It does not certify that a business's earnings are permanently sustainable, determine an agreed purchase price or guarantee that the buyer should close.

  • Agree the scope
  • Reconcile a fictional earnings bridge
  • Separate earnings and cash
  • Check lender requirements
  • Resolve findings

Agree what the engagement will test

Baker Tilly's QoE guidance distinguishes a consulting engagement from an audit and discusses source support for adjustments, cash proof, revenue recognition and working capital. Its guidance also explains why EBITDA is an imperfect substitute for cash available to stakeholders.

Ask the provider to specify the purpose, intended users, covered entities and periods, earnings measure, procedures, records required, exclusions, deliverables and unresolved-data treatment. Confirm independence and relevant experience. Coordinate with the lender before assuming a buyer-commissioned or seller-commissioned report will satisfy its requirements.

Obtain a scoped fee proposal rather than relying on a universal price range or a percentage of purchase price. Timing depends on access, readiness, financing and the transaction's conditions. Plan the work before committing to a deadline that depends on findings not yet available. Financial diligence does not replace legal, tax, commercial, operational or technology review.

Trace each proposed adjustment to evidence

Original fictional exercise. Assume the same $500,000 reported EBITDA starting point for both columns below. The seller proposes two add-backs. A hypothetical review supports only portions of them and identifies an incremental management cost not already reflected in reported earnings. The figures are invented; they are not a typical reduction, a provider's finding or an acquisition valuation.

The seller column is $500,000 + $60,000 + $40,000 = $600,000. The review column is $500,000 + $40,000 + $30,000 − $35,000 = $535,000. The difference comprises $20,000 of unsupported fee adjustment, $10,000 of recurring owner-related cost and $35,000 of incremental management cost. Each row has one role in the calculation; do not deduct a replacement cost twice.

Download the fictional earnings bridge (CSV). Five rows include both signed amounts, the assumed evidence and a blank reader-review column. The total row is a result, not another input. Actual source records and a defined earnings measure are required before applying the approach.

For a real adjustment, record the ledger account, period, source document, reason, proposed amount, review conclusion and any unresolved question. An invoice shows a payment or obligation; its label alone does not establish that the cost will disappear. An owner-related cost may still be necessary under new ownership. A buyer's intended operating model also needs explicit support.

Do not substitute the earnings total for cash

Review receipts and disbursements against the underlying reporting, including timing differences. Cash deposits are not automatically current-period revenue, and recorded revenue is not automatically collected cash. Identify receivables, customer deposits, unpaid obligations, inventory and other reconciling items before interpreting a difference.

Keep the earnings bridge, working-capital analysis, debt-like items, capital expenditure and cash forecast distinct and reconciled. A QoE finding does not by itself set the contractual working-capital target or change what the purchase agreement includes. Use the working-capital review and 13-week cash exercise to develop the separate records. A $535,000 earnings result is not $535,000 available for debt service or owner distributions.

Apply the actual SBA requirement when relevant

The current SBA SOP 50 10 8.1 with technical updates, Appendix 15, requires a lender QoE in addition to business valuation for Initial Acquisition and Business Expansion transactions with a defined Business Purchase Price of at least $3 million. The threshold is assessed before buyer equity, seller debt or other financing sources. Owner Buyout and ESOP/cooperative categories are not subject to that QoE requirement.

The SOP requires an independent, experienced professional's report for the lender's benefit; it cannot be prepared by or for the seller. A buyer's existing report requires the lender's vendor review before reliance. Cash proof covers the trailing 12 months and last two fiscal years, with a reduced period possible for a business operating less than two years. Confirm the category, defined price, scope and applicable notices with the lender. See the SBA acquisition guide.

Decide what a finding changes

Separate a documented adjustment from an unanswered question, missing record or sensitivity. Discuss material issues with the responsible advisers and financing parties. Possible next steps include further work, a revised model, negotiating terms, retaining conditions or declining the transaction; a negative adjustment is not automatically negotiating leverage or a required reason to proceed.

Do not turn an earnings difference into a price change without considering the valuation assumptions and negotiated terms. Any seller note, escrow or contingent price must be reviewed for its actual purpose and financing constraints. In particular, the SBA change-of-ownership rules prohibit seller earnouts; a QoE issue does not create an exception.

Continue with the earnings and valuation review, funding records and holdback review. These website resources do not establish that a book contains a particular template or that a report will recover its cost.
