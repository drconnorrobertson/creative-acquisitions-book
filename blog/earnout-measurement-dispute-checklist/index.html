Acquisition Earnouts: Define the Measurement Before the Price

Review earnout metrics, accounting consistency, operating control, reporting, and dispute procedures before depending on contingent acquisition payments.

An earnout can bridge a disagreement about future performance, but it also creates a measurement problem. If buyer and seller mean different things by revenue or profit, the headline purchase price conceals a future dispute. Define the measurement process before negotiating the maximum payout.

Choose a metric both parties can reproduce

A useful metric has a clear source, measurement period, and calculation method. Revenue may look simple, but the agreement still needs to address recognition timing, refunds, credits, uncollected invoices, related-party sales, and work performed across the measurement boundary. Profit metrics introduce additional cost-allocation questions.

Have the accountant produce a sample calculation from historical records. Ask both sides to calculate the same period independently using the proposed definition. Differences reveal ambiguity while there is still time to fix it.

Use an example that includes an adjustment

Suppose a fictional proposal pays the seller 20% of eligible revenue above $1 million, subject to a $100,000 cap. Reported revenue is $1.4 million. The preliminary payout is $80,000. If $100,000 of that revenue must be excluded under the agreed definition, eligible revenue is $1.3 million and the payout becomes $60,000.

The $20,000 difference comes from the definition, not the percentage. State the treatment of the disputed category directly rather than relying on a broad label such as ordinary accounting practice.

Match responsibility with control

List the decisions that affect the metric: pricing, staffing, marketing, credit terms, product changes, customer allocation, and shared overhead. Identify who controls each after closing. The seller may depend on buyer decisions; the buyer may need freedom to integrate the business. Those interests should be addressed explicitly through the transaction documents.

For a profit-based metric, test what happens when the buyer hires a replacement manager or allocates central-office costs. For a revenue-based metric, test whether low-margin discounts could increase the payout while reducing the business's cash. Neither metric is automatically fair in every situation.

Design reporting and dispute handling

Agree on reporting frequency, supporting records, delivery deadlines, review rights, objection periods, and how a calculation dispute will be resolved. Ask counsel to distinguish an accounting disagreement from a broader contractual dispute. Do not leave the process to an informal promise to work it out later.

The buyer should model the maximum payout and its due date in the cash forecast. The seller should evaluate the uncertainty of contingent proceeds. Both should obtain transaction-specific tax and legal advice on the structure.

Six fictional threshold, exclusion, and cap cases

This original website exercise extends the example above. It is not a verified book excerpt, a client transaction, a valuation, or approved contract language. Assume one measurement period, a $1,000,000 threshold, a 20 percent rate on eligible revenue above that threshold, a zero floor, and a $100,000 payout cap. These are fictional proposal conditions, not standard earnout terms.

Exercise formula: eligible revenue = reported revenue − assumed agreed exclusions. Uncapped payout = 20% × max(eligible revenue − $1,000,000, $0). Final modeled payout = min(uncapped payout, $100,000).

A zero floor prevents a below-threshold calculation from becoming a negative payout. A cap limits the modeled payout after the percentage is applied. This formula measures one assumed component; it does not determine an actual contractual amount due or payment date.

Below and exactly at the threshold

E01's eligible revenue is $50,000 below the threshold. Under this exercise's zero floor, payout is $0, not −$10,000. E02 reaches exactly $1,000,000, leaving no excess, so payout is also $0. A different agreement could use a milestone or a different threshold convention; the word “threshold” alone does not establish this formula.

An exclusion changes the result before the cap

E03 and E04 hold reported revenue at $1,400,000. E03 includes all of it, producing $80,000. E04 assumes the agreed definition excludes $100,000, producing eligible revenue of $1,300,000 and a $60,000 payout. The $20,000 difference reproduces the existing example above. The exclusion is assumed for the exercise; it is not automatically required by an accounting label.

The cap changes the marginal effect

E05 reaches the $100,000 cap at $1,500,000 eligible revenue. E06's uncapped result is $160,000, but its modeled payout remains $100,000. An extra $300,000 of eligible revenue between those rows produces no additional modeled payout under this cap.

If E06 instead excluded $100,000, eligible revenue would be $1,700,000 and the uncapped amount $140,000. The capped payout would still be $100,000. A disputed category can therefore change the calculation without changing the capped payout in that particular case. That does not decide the category's contractual treatment or whether it matters for other periods or provisions.

Reconcile the metric before recording a payment conclusion

  1. Identify the controlling metric definition, period boundaries, threshold convention, rate, floor, cap, and any period-specific or aggregate limits.
  2. Trace reported revenue to source records and each proposed exclusion to the actual agreed definition. Record disputed items separately rather than silently applying them.
  3. Show eligible revenue, threshold excess, floor, percentage, and cap as separate steps. Check whether caps apply by year, in aggregate, or through another mechanism.
  4. Record the preparer, independent reviewer, calculation version, supporting records, and unresolved differences.
  5. Review the actual reporting, objection, settlement, and payment requirements with the transaction advisers. A model result is not proof that a payment is presently due.

For a primary-source illustration of different drafting, section 2.7 of the October 28, 2025 RG Beck AZ/Safariland securities purchase agreement filed with the SEC specifies annual and aggregate caps and a tiered revenue formula. Its conditions differ from this fictional exercise; do not substitute either formula for an actual agreement.

Do not infer an earnout payment's tax treatment, enforceability, financing eligibility, or a right to withhold another payment from these arithmetic cases. Keep operating-control and dispute questions in the review described above.
