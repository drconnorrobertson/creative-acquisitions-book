The Personal Guarantee Playbook: Negotiating Limits, Carve-Outs, and Release Triggers

A personal guarantee creates obligations that need review alongside the acquisition loan and collateral documents. Do not assume a guarantee is limited to an ownership percentage, that business assets must be exhausted first, or that a lender will release it after a period of good performance. Read the actual form and applicable program requirements before negotiating or signing.

  • Check SBA requirements
  • Read the actual obligations
  • Compare document states
  • Verify a release
  • Review signatures and exposure

SBA requirements limit negotiation

The SBA Form 148 page states that individuals owning 20% or more of an applicant must provide an unlimited personal guaranty. The current SOP addresses direct and indirect ownership and other required guarantors. Ownership below 20% is not, by itself, proof that no guarantee is required.

Form 148 provides for payment on written demand without first seeking payment elsewhere, joint and several liability, enforcement expenses and continuing liability even if SBA pays the lender. It also says oral statements cannot contradict the written terms. Review the form actually proposed; 7(a) lenders may use an equivalent form.

These provisions are not a menu of standard caps or automatic releases. A proposed limit must comply with the program and be accepted by the authorized parties. Conventional or seller financing requires separate document review; do not infer that a concession available in one transaction is available in another.

Build a clause-by-clause review record

  • Who is obligated? List each borrower, guarantor, pledgor and signing entity. A partner's ownership percentage is not the same as a contractual liability cap.
  • Which debt is covered? Identify the note and whether renewals, substitutions, future advances or other obligations are included.
  • What triggers payment? Locate demand, default, notice, cure and collection provisions. Do not assume a creditor must pursue the company first.
  • What is limited? If a cap is proposed, identify whether interest, costs and enforcement expenses are inside or outside it and what can change the limit.
  • What is waived? Ask counsel to explain notices, defenses, lender powers and the interaction with other loan documents.
  • What evidence changes the obligation? Record the approval, amendment or release required and who has authority to provide it.

Read any carve-out or expansion provision in full. Do not sign fraud, environmental, insurance or other clauses on the assumption that a familiar label makes their scope harmless. Identify the conduct, responsible persons, causation, remedy and any cure right. The correct treatment depends on the actual language and law.

A file can contain strong operating results and still lack a release. Keep correspondence separate from executed instruments and record each unresolved condition. A proposed clause, satisfaction of a test and an effective release are different review states.

Ask what ends or changes the obligation

A scheduled principal reduction, ownership transfer or refinancing should not be treated as proof that a guarantee has ended. Review the surviving debt, amendments, substitution requirements and any release approval with the lender and counsel. Do not present a 24-month, 48-month or debt-service-coverage target as a standard entitlement.

If a limited guarantee is genuinely permitted and offered, have counsel reconcile the cap, duration, payment credits, expense treatment, expansion triggers and release mechanism. A separate agreement among co-owners about sharing costs does not itself amend a lender's rights. Retain final documents and confirmation of any effective release.

Identify the purpose of every signature

For each requested spousal or third-party signature, establish whether the person signs as borrower, guarantor, collateral owner or in another capacity. Ask counsel to explain the proposed obligation and applicable state law. Do not assume that an acknowledgment, lien consent and guarantee have the same effect, or that a particular asset is automatically protected.

Discuss lawful planning before committing to terms. Do not move or retitle assets to evade existing obligations. Bring ownership records, debt documents, proposed guarantees, collateral schedules, relevant amendments and household questions to the appropriate advisers.
