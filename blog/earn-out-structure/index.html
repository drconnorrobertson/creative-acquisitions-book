Acquisition Earnouts: Formula, Records, and Financing Limits

An acquisition earnout makes an additional amount of consideration depend on defined post-closing conditions. Its value to either party depends on the actual formula, operating rights, records, funding restrictions, and payment process. A future target does not guarantee an additional payment, a fair price, or a successful transition.

This website guide was reviewed November 22, 2026. It is a review of proposed provisions, not a contract template, completed transaction, valuation opinion, or statement that an earnout is permitted under a particular financing program.

  • Check financing eligibility first
  • Define the measurement
  • Trace the records and review process
  • Review operating control
  • Test boundaries and exclusions

Check financing eligibility before designing a formula

For SBA-financed ownership changes, Appendix 15 of the current SOP 50 10 8.1, effective October 1, 2026, prohibits seller earnouts. It distinguishes buyer rebates and working-capital adjustments. A general acquisition discussion does not override those rules. Have the lender and transaction counsel classify any proposed contingent provision before including it in a financing plan.

In other transactions, review the purchase agreement and financing documents for the actual permitted structure and restrictions. A contingent purchase-price obligation is not automatically the same instrument as a seller promissory note. Do not assume a priority, security interest, deduction, payment right, or ability to fund it from business cash.

Define the measurement before evaluating the payout

  • Metric: identify the exact revenue, profit, customer, or other measure. Its label alone does not resolve recognition, refunds, intercompany items, allocations, or exclusions.
  • Period: specify the start and end dates, partial periods, cutoffs, and treatment of later adjustments.
  • Formula: distinguish a threshold, milestone, percentage above a threshold, tiered calculation, floor, annual cap, and aggregate cap.
  • Amount and timing: separate a calculated amount from when it becomes payable and which unresolved reviews can affect it.
  • Boundaries: test values just below, at, and above each threshold and cap. Identify the effect of disputed categories before drawing a conclusion.

Revenue is not inherently simple or immune to disputes. Profit measures introduce different cost and allocation questions. Choose and define a measure through transaction review rather than assuming one metric always protects both parties.

Make the result reproducible

Record the controlling definition, calculation version, source records, adjustments, preparer, and reviewer. Show proposed exclusions and disputed amounts separately. Identify who prepares the calculation, what information the other party receives, how an objection is made, and the applicable deadlines and resolution process in the agreement.

For a primary-source example of distinct drafting, section 2.7 of the October 28, 2025 RG Beck AZ/Safariland agreement filed with the SEC uses a tiered revenue calculation with annual and aggregate caps. It illustrates one agreement, not a standard formula or terms suitable for another acquisition.

Review control and performance dependencies

Identify who controls pricing, customer acceptance, staffing, spending, integrations, accounting policies, and changes to the acquired operation during the measurement period. Review what the actual agreement permits or restricts, and how an operating change affects the measure. A payout formula alone does not establish continuing seller involvement or constrain the buyer's conduct.

Discuss the consequences of lost customers, delayed collections, unexpected costs, reporting changes, business resale, and disputes with the responsible advisers. The appropriate period and protections depend on the transaction; this guide does not prescribe a universal two- or three-year term.

Use separate worked cases to test the proposal

The six-case earnout exercise uses a fictional $1 million threshold, a 20% rate on excess eligible revenue, a zero floor, and a $100,000 cap. It shows how an exclusion can change an uncapped calculation yet leave a capped payout unchanged. Those assumptions are not terms of the SEC agreement or an actual proposed acquisition.

The linked guide includes the worked-case CSV and a separate blank review checklist. Use them to expose questions, not to establish an amount legally due, tax treatment, financing eligibility, or a right to withhold another payment.
