Partner Capital: Proposed Funding vs. Closing Cash

Separate the amount discussed, the conditions still open, and the cash treated as available before calling an acquisition funding plan complete.

Reviewed November 22, 2026Creative Acquisitions website guide

This is an original fictional website exercise, not a client acquisition, verified book excerpt, investment offer, bank confirmation, or approved funding package. The amounts, packet records, and availability treatments below are invented assumptions for a review exercise. They do not authorize a transfer, confirm an investment commitment, or establish that a real acquisition can close.

The fictional requirement is $750,000

Assume the review covers $600,000 of seller cash consideration, $50,000 of transaction cash costs, and $100,000 of initial operating cash to remain in the business. Total cash uses are $750,000. These are assumed amounts, not a fee benchmark, valuation, or complete assessment of a real business's liquidity needs.

The initial funding plan lists $500,000 of senior borrowing, $100,000 of operator cash, and $150,000 of partner equity. Planned sources also total $750,000. That arithmetic balances the proposal; it does not show that each amount is presently available under the required conditions.

The packet supports a different cash total

For this exercise, use the specific availability treatment in each fictional packet record. “Treated as available” is a model assumption, not a statement that a real bank, investor, lawyer, or closing agent verified funds. Do not put credentials or payment instructions into this worksheet.

The amount treated as available is $400,000 + $100,000 + $100,000 = $600,000. Against $750,000 of uses, the funding gap is $150,000. It equals the $100,000 borrowing difference plus the $50,000 partner difference. Do not count either unresolved amount again as available cash.

A conversation, a draft term sheet, an agreement, a funding condition, and a cleared cash record answer different questions. The legal force and permissible use of actual documents and funds require review. Do not infer from this packet that a signature guarantees funding or that a cash balance satisfies every closing condition.

Build a source-by-source review record

  • Amount and date: record the proposed amount, currency, modeled funding date, available amount, and any expiry or timing constraint.
  • Entity and permitted use: identify who supplies funds, who receives them, and which uses are permitted under the actual documents.
  • Document status: distinguish discussions, drafts, signed documents, open conditions, and the actual evidence used to verify funding availability.
  • Dependencies: identify required consents, approvals, source-of-funds review, matching contributions, escrow arrangements, or other relevant conditions.
  • Cash reconciliation: show each verified source once, each use once, the unresolved difference, the responsible reviewer, and the next action.
  • Changes: update the funding and uses together when fees, working-cash needs, price adjustments, or timing assumptions change. Preserve the prior version rather than silently replacing its assumptions.

Funding share is not ownership or control

A partner's planned dollar contribution does not establish a percentage of the acquisition entity. Review what security or interest is issued, its unit price, the full capitalization, distribution priority, voting and consent rights, future funding provisions, transfer restrictions, and exit terms. A lender's advance is also not automatically common equity.

The rollover ownership guide separately illustrates why a contribution amount and a new ownership percentage can use different denominators. Keep that capitalization analysis distinct from this cash-availability review.

The SEC's private-placement investor bulletin explains that private placements can be illiquid and that a Form D filing does not represent SEC approval. This guide does not determine whether a proposed partner interest is a security, whether an exemption is available, or whether any offering or investor satisfies legal requirements. Have the responsible advisers review the actual arrangement.

A balanced cash model does not establish investment suitability, legal authority, tax treatment, securities compliance, loan eligibility, the right to use another person's funds, or operational readiness. Record those as separate review questions.

Questions about the exercise

Why is a balanced funding plan not enough?

In the fictional packet, planned sources total $750,000 but only $600,000 is treated as available under the stated assumptions. The $150,000 difference remains a funding gap until the missing amounts and their conditions are resolved.

Does a zero funding gap approve the acquisition?

No. It reconciles one cash requirement under stated assumptions. It does not establish legal authority, securities compliance, lender approval, document completion, investment suitability, or operational readiness.

Does the partner contribution determine ownership?

The funding amount alone does not determine ownership or control. Review the security, entity, unit price, capitalization, economic rights, governance, and governing documents separately.
